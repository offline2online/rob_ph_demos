# Thailand

**Region:** Southeast Asia. **Business English:** UK spelling. **Currency:** Thai baht (THB).

This page sets out how Personalisation Hub writes and speaks to retailers in Thailand: one-pagers, decks, emails and replies. The general Brand Guidelines still apply to everything here (logo, colour, typography, the message house). This page only adds what changes for a Thai audience, on top of the industry, partner and purchase-stage messaging.

## Communication style

- **Indirect and polite.** Thai business culture values *kreng jai* (consideration for the other person) and saving face. Suggest rather than instruct, and never put a reader in a position where they must say no in public.
- **Formal by default.** Address people as "Khun" with their first name (Khun Somchai), in writing and in person. Keep that form until they set a different tone.
- **Respect seniority.** Write to the most senior person with deference, acknowledge their position, and copy their team rather than going around them. Decisions are made at the top.
- **Soften problems.** Describe a gap as an opportunity ("there is room to make every store visit worth more") rather than a failure of the retailer's current approach.
- **Warm, not familiar.** Thank the reader for their time, keep humour gentle, and avoid slang, sarcasm and idioms that do not translate.

## Cultural sensitivities

- **The monarchy.** Never use images of the King or the royal family, royal symbols, or any reference to them in marketing. Lèse-majesté is a criminal offence under Section 112 of the Criminal Code, and the bar is very low. No jokes, no comparisons, no "king of" phrases about products.
- **Religion.** Never use Buddha images or monks as decoration or in advertising, and never place a Buddha image low, on the floor or next to products. Temples appear only respectfully and only when relevant.
- **Head and feet.** The head is respected and the feet are low. No imagery of feet pointing at people, shoes on tables or touching someone's head.
- **Colours.** Black is associated with mourning: keep it as a surface colour (the dark surface of the brand), not a theme for celebratory pieces. Avoid using red or yellow as a political statement; the brand palette as it stands is fine.
- **Gestures.** The *wai* (palms together) is the greeting; do not show it done casually or as a joke. Do not show pointing with a finger or foot.
- **Politics.** Stay out of it entirely, including colours or slogans tied to political movements.

## Local conventions

- **Spelling:** UK English for English-language material (personalise, colour, centre, optimise). Thai-language material is translated by a native speaker, never machine translated.
- **Currency:** Thai baht. Write "THB 1,500" in tables and formal material, "฿1,500" in short copy. No decimals for whole amounts.
- **Dates:** day, month, year (19 November 2026, or 19/11/2026). Official Thai documents use the Buddhist Era (2569 BE for 2026); English-language collateral uses the Gregorian year.
- **Numbers:** comma for thousands, point for decimals (12,500.75).
- **Units:** metric (metres, kilograms, °C).
- **Time:** 24-hour clock in written schedules (14:30).

## Business etiquette

- **Relationship first.** Expect an introduction and a conversation before a proposal. A referral from someone they know carries more weight than any cold message.
- **Follow up gently.** Allow three to five working days before a polite follow-up, and do not chase more than once a week. Silence is often a polite no or an internal decision still in progress.
- **Soft sell.** Lead with the shared goal and proof from similar retailers in the region. Avoid pressure, countdowns, "last chance" wording and hard closing language.
- **In person matters.** Offer a meeting or a store visit rather than pushing for a decision by email. LINE is the common business messaging app; ask before moving a conversation there.
- **Gifts and hospitality** are part of relationship building, but keep them modest and within the retailer's own gifting policy.

## Regulatory notes

- **Personal data:** the Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 (2019, PDPA) requires a lawful basis, usually consent, for marketing use of personal data, clear purpose notices, and safeguards for transfers outside Thailand. Say that shopper data stays within the retailer's own AWS account and region.
- **Advertising claims:** the Consumer Protection Act requires claims to be true and provable; the Office of the Consumer Protection Board can order changes. Only use outcomes from the industry pages on personalisationhub.com.
- **Health and pharmacy:** advertising of medicines, supplements and health products needs approval from the Thai FDA, and claims are tightly limited. Never suggest a health outcome in pharmacy collateral.
- **Alcohol:** alcohol advertising is heavily restricted under the Alcoholic Beverage Control Act. Do not show alcohol products or brands in examples for Thai retailers.
- **Comparative advertising:** avoid naming or disparaging a competitor.

3. Open with a courteous greeting using "Khun" and the first name. Frame the value as a shared goal, keep the call to action an invitation (a conversation or a store visit), and never use urgency language.
4. Run a final check: no royal, Buddhist or political imagery or references, no feet, no alcohol, no health outcome claims, no competitor named.

### Typical phrasing

- Opening: "Dear Khun Somchai, thank you for your time last week."
- Call to action: "We would be glad to visit a store with your team and show how this could work for you."
