# Singapore

**Region:** Southeast Asia. **Business English:** UK spelling. **Currency:** Singapore dollar (SGD).

This page sets out how Personalisation Hub writes and speaks to retailers in Singapore: one-pagers, decks, emails and replies. The general Brand Guidelines still apply to everything here. This page only adds what changes for a Singapore audience, on top of the industry, partner and purchase-stage messaging.

## Communication style

- **Clear and efficient.** Singapore business readers expect the point early, backed by numbers. Be more direct than in Thailand, but stay courteous.
- **Formal at first.** Use "Mr", "Ms" or "Dr" with the family name until invited to use first names. Chinese names usually put the family name first (Mr Tan for Tan Wei Ming).
- **Respect seniority.** Address the senior decision maker, acknowledge their role, and keep their team copied. Avoid contradicting a senior person in front of others.
- **Plain, international English.** English is the language of business. Avoid local slang (Singlish) in written material even if the reader uses it, and avoid idioms that do not travel.
- **Calm confidence.** Show results and a clear plan; avoid hype and superlatives.

## Cultural sensitivities

- **Race and religion.** Singapore is multiracial and multireligious (Chinese, Malay, Indian and others). Never use racial or religious stereotypes, jokes or comparisons; the Maintenance of Religious Harmony Act and the advertising code both take this seriously. Show diverse shoppers in imagery.
- **Food and halal.** In grocery and food examples for a broad audience, avoid pork and alcohol in hero imagery, and never show them next to halal products.
- **Numbers and colours.** The number four sounds like "death" in Chinese dialects: avoid it as a featured number (a "4-step plan" reads better as three or five). White and black are associated with funerals in Chinese culture; keep celebratory pieces to the brand colours. Red and gold read as good fortune.
- **Gestures.** Point with the whole hand, not a finger. Hand over a business card with both hands.
- **Politics.** Stay out of local politics and government commentary.

## Local conventions

- **Spelling:** UK English (personalise, colour, centre, optimise).
- **Currency:** Singapore dollar. Write "S$1,500" in short copy and "SGD 1,500" in tables and formal material; always distinguish it from US dollars.
- **Dates:** day, month, year (19 November 2026, or 19/11/2026).
- **Numbers:** comma for thousands, point for decimals (12,500.75).
- **Units:** metric (metres, kilograms, °C).
- **Time:** 12-hour (2.30pm) or 24-hour (14:30) are both understood; be consistent within a piece.

## Business etiquette

- **Prompt and punctual.** Reply within one working day and follow up after two or three working days if there is no answer. Confirm meetings in writing with a short agenda.
- **Evidence-led selling.** Lead with return on investment, measured outcomes and how the platform fits governance and security requirements. A firm, clear next step is welcome; pressure is not.
- **Relationships still matter,** especially with family-owned groups, but the first meeting can be about the business. Exchange business cards with both hands and read them before putting them away.
- **Gifts:** many companies, and every public-sector body, restrict gifts. Do not offer anything beyond modest hospitality.

## Regulatory notes

- **Personal data:** the Personal Data Protection Act 2012 (PDPA) requires consent and a stated purpose for collecting and using personal data, and the Do Not Call registry must be checked before marketing calls, SMS or faxes to Singapore numbers.
- **Electronic marketing:** the Spam Control Act requires an unsubscribe facility and accurate sender details in commercial email and SMS, and SMS that is unsolicited must carry an `<ADV>` label.
- **Advertising claims:** the Singapore Code of Advertising Practice (ASAS) requires claims to be legal, decent, honest and substantiated; the Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act covers misleading claims. Only use outcomes from the industry pages on personalisationhub.com.
- **Health and pharmacy:** health product and medicine advertising is controlled under the Health Products Act and the Medicines Act (HSA). Never suggest a health outcome in pharmacy collateral.

### Typical phrasing

- Opening: "Dear Mr Tan, thank you for meeting us on Tuesday."
- Call to action: "Could we book 30 minutes next week to walk your team through a pilot plan?"
