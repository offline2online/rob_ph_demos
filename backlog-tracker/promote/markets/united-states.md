# United States

**Region:** North America. **Business English:** US spelling. **Currency:** US dollar (USD).

This page sets out how Personalisation Hub writes and speaks to retailers in the United States: one-pagers, decks, emails and replies. The general Brand Guidelines still apply to everything here. This page only adds what changes for a US audience, on top of the industry, partner and purchase-stage messaging.

## Communication style

- **Direct and concise.** Lead with the outcome and the number, then the how. Short sentences, short paragraphs, one idea each.
- **First names early.** Use the reader's first name from the first email unless they sign with a title (Dr) or the setting is clearly formal.
- **Less deference.** Write to the decision maker as a peer. Titles matter less than the problem you solve for them.
- **Confident, not hyped.** State what the platform does and what it has delivered. Avoid unproven superlatives ("the best", "guaranteed"): they are a legal risk as well as a tone problem.
- **Action oriented.** Every piece ends with a specific next step.

## Cultural sensitivities

- **Politics and religion.** Keep both out of marketing entirely, including holidays framed in a religious way; "the holiday season" is the safe phrase.
- **Inclusive imagery.** Show shoppers and staff of different ages, ethnicities, abilities and body types. Avoid stereotypes in copy and imagery.
- **Accessibility.** Digital material follows accessible design (contrast, alt text, readable type sizes); the Americans with Disabilities Act is applied to websites and digital experiences.
- **Sensitive data.** US readers are alert to privacy: say plainly what is and is not collected about shoppers.
- **Symbols and gestures.** Avoid weapons, war metaphors and imagery tied to political movements. Hand gestures carry little risk, but keep them neutral.

## Local conventions

- **Spelling:** US English: personalize, color, center, optimize, catalog, program, behavior, analyze. "Personalisation Hub" is a proper name and keeps its spelling.
- **Currency:** US dollar. Write "$1,500", or "USD 1,500" where other currencies appear on the same page.
- **Dates:** month, day, year. Spell the month (November 19, 2026) to avoid confusion with day-first formats; if numeric, 11/19/2026.
- **Numbers:** comma for thousands, point for decimals (12,500.75).
- **Units:** imperial first (feet, square feet, miles, °F), with metric in brackets where it helps.
- **Time:** 12-hour clock with AM and PM, and the time zone (2:30 PM ET).

## Business etiquette

- **Fast follow-up.** Reply within one business day, send a recap with next steps the same day as a meeting, and follow up after two or three business days of silence.
- **Get straight to the point.** Small talk is short; the first meeting is about the business problem and the return on investment.
- **A clear ask is welcome.** It is normal to propose a pilot, a timeline and a decision date. Be direct, but never use false urgency.
- **Respect time.** Keep meetings to the booked length and send material in advance.
- **Gifts:** keep hospitality modest; public companies and many retailers have strict gift policies.

## Regulatory notes

- **Advertising claims:** Section 5 of the FTC Act prohibits deceptive claims; every objective claim needs substantiation before it is published. Only use outcomes from the industry pages on personalisationhub.com, and keep their source.
- **Endorsements:** the FTC Endorsement Guides require any material connection (a partner, a paid customer, free product) to be disclosed next to a testimonial.
- **Email:** CAN-SPAM requires an honest subject line, a clear unsubscribe that works within ten business days, and a valid physical postal address.
- **SMS and calls:** the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) requires prior express written consent for marketing texts and automated calls.
- **Privacy:** state laws apply, led by California's CCPA as amended by the CPRA (notice at collection, the right to opt out of sale or sharing, limits on sensitive data), with similar laws in Colorado, Virginia, Connecticut, Texas and other states. Children's data falls under COPPA.
- **Health and pharmacy:** health information from pharmacies can fall under HIPAA; never suggest using prescription data for targeting, and never claim a health outcome.
